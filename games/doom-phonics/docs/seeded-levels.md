# Seeded layered levels

The seeded generator creates a separate `SEEDED.WAD`: three planar room networks connected by a flooded ledge and a spiral staircase. The default seed is `first-light`, with a budget of fourteen room candidates per layer (forty-two across the three storeys). The original `PORTALS.WAD` demonstrations remain separate.

[Play the generated level](/games/doom-phonics/?world=/engine/packages/doom-phonics/demo/seeded-level.phonics-world.json), or inspect its [interactive navigation report](/engine/packages/doom-phonics/demo/seeded-navigation.html). Activate the game poster, choose **New Game**, and select a skill. You can also stack [SEEDED.WAD](/engine/packages/doom-phonics/demo/SEEDED.WAD) above `DOOM1.WAD` and select its `SEEDED` map.

## How to explore

Start in the basement and find its tall pool chamber. The recessed computer switch on its east wall fills the basin; the recessed brown switch drains it. Two raised ledge chambers overlook the basin and stay dry even when it is full. Their connecting north landing provides an exit, and draining the pool does not trap someone on either ledge. Another drain/refill pair serves the upper ledge, so controls remain accessible at water level. Each alcove has its own floor and ceiling framing the 32 × 32 button patch. Press **E** or controller **A / Cross** to use either switch. Filling also floods connected low rooms and hallways. Look upward and swim forward to reach the chamber's north ledge; its passage leads to the ground floor.

Explore the branching rooms and find the curved staircase to the upper floor. Every tread turns gradually through a half-circle in organic layouts, or a seeded quarter- or half-circle in building layouts, with the inner arc clipped away to form a broad curved stair hall. Suitable rooms place their entrances and exits at different heights. Broad stairs connect opposing landings, with side entrances on a flat middle landing. Some two-entrance rooms use a quarter-turn corner staircase instead: walk into its lower landing, climb around the corner, and follow the raised wall ledge to the higher exit. Metal lift faces can be used from their lower or upper landing: call the lift, board while it is down, and wait for it to rise. The upper call remains repeatable, and the doorway has enough clearance to leave with the platform raised. The central chamber in the upper circular hub contains a recessed floor portal back into the pool chamber. Its matching ceiling portal is visible from below; the flooded ledge and curved stairs provide a return route. Keys and locks are not implemented yet. Collect the soulsphere in the upper network as the exploration goal. This prototype has no combat population or exit switch.

Additional 192-unit-wide drop shafts connect ordinary rooms on adjacent storeys: two ground-to-basement pairs and two upper-to-ground pairs in the default map, alongside the hub-to-pool portal and a two-link gallery stack. Floor openings sit 32 units below the surrounding floor, with an intermediate 16-unit escape ledge and clear walkways around all four sides and matching ceiling views in the rooms below. Spawn, goal and moving lifts stay outside these apertures. Smaller room counts use fewer sites. The flooded ledge and spiral remain the upward route, so these drops create shortcuts without trapping the player. Orange numbered markers in the report identify both ends of each pair.

A three-level gallery follows the stacked-room construction from POOL3D. Its 384-unit aperture lets you drop through the middle gallery to the basement without climbing back up. Each gallery rim connects to its own layer network, and the lower level has a return route through the wider map. `--gallery-halls=all` surrounds each gallery with hallways along all four sides, separated by 64-unit solid walls with four openings. `--gallery-halls=none` leaves an open rim; the default `seeded` choice varies by level. Small layouts without a suitable room on every layer omit this optional stack.

The report shows room placement, layer heights, and every repair made during generation. Change the water state or disable pump actions to see which routes become inaccessible. The graph tracks individual cores, ledges, treads, landings and passages, rather than assuming every door in a room connects to every other door.

The ground cluster includes a sky-lit enclosed courtyard and an edge garden that interrupts its concentric shape. The garden's lowered ground and ledges have directed drop/stair connections, just like interior rooms. A later generator can attach a sewer route to those lower regions; this version does not add a separate sewer network.

Room elevations follow their connected passage heights. Wide enclosed stair hallways connect ordinary entrances at different heights in monotonic 16-unit steps. Selected large rooms have a broad upper ledge with a staircase cut into one side, turning along its perimeter; both upper and lower floors remain usable. The generator reserves the passage anchors and verifies routes between every entrance and exit. Suitable large rooms can have a substantial lowered center with a broad perimeter ledge and a traversable return step. The report lists internal entrance-to-exit paths and one-way crossings separately for each water state.

## Generate another seed

Run from the repository root:

```sh
php packages/doom-phonics/tools/generate-seeded.php --seed=cavern-42 --rooms=6
npm run build:manifests
node scripts/build-docs.mjs
npm run build:site
```

`--rooms` accepts 2–16 requested candidates per layer. It is a placement budget: cuts can delete candidates, split disconnected remnants, or merge several candidates into one logical room. The CLI and report list the requested candidates and surviving rooms separately. Seeds are exact strings; `42` and `042` differ. Generator **version 13** records all input options and explicit dry, shallow and full water heights in `GENINFO`. Identical inputs produce identical WAD, world and report bytes.

Organic generation starts with an **embedded planar graph for each layer**. Node reservations keep connections planar before room outlines are fitted. One to three anchor nodes receive large area targets, including the existing special structures. Ordinary anchors target 1,024–1,536 units; surrounding nodes target 384–896 units and shrink with distance from the nearest anchor boundary. Independent attachment offsets replace shared grid rows and columns. The embedding favors contacts that preserve branches and loops. `--layout=building` retains the rectangular building generator.

Rooms conform to that graph. Rectangular candidates use one, two, or three overlapping rectangles. Multi-rectangle footprints combine horizontal and vertical proportions to form L, T, cross and stepped outlines; faceted circular anchors remain in the mix. A bounded fitting pass preserves a continuous shared entrance span on both neighbors, falling back to a single rectangle when needed. The report records each candidate’s source rectangles before subsequent cuts and merges. Their convex pieces share logical ownership, so decomposition seams stay open. Shared boundaries produce both wall faces through a common offset; there is no independent organic-room shearing. All final vertices must be integral WAD coordinates. Selected merges retain concave outlines and the 1,835,008-square-unit combined area limit. Overlapping gallery reservations can trim, replace or split several neighboring rooms. Narrow fragments are merged, removed, or omitted when no full-width entrance survives. Optional joins that disconnect after wall offsets are reverted.

Independent seeded X/Y directions define a 16-unit-quantized elevation target with a requested rise of 128–256 units. Ordinary elevation changes use enclosed connecting hallways with 256-unit-wide stairs, 32-unit solid side walls, a 256-unit top landing and a level lower approach. The main room floors stay level around these hallways. Suitable large rooms can instead contain a broad raised ledge, 96–128 units above the lower floor, with two stair flights recessed into its perimeter and a full turning landing. Conflicting elevation regions coalesce deterministically; the report distinguishes requested targets from the resulting room heights. Basins, recessed apertures and lowered centers are explicit departures from the elevation trend. Large suitable rooms receive one treatment: a solid central pillar, an incomplete regular pillar group, or a substantial lowered center. Pillars are voids bounded by solid map walls; depressed centers retain a perimeter ledge and a directly traversable return step.

The ground cluster starts with an outline: the union of a broad octagonal body and two tapered wings within a 2,400-unit span. Its exterior retains concave bays instead of filling a rectangular or convex container. An octagonal core and eight surrounding partitions initially pack that union. A seeded cardinal or diagonal polygon then cuts across the core and its neighbors, replacing the interrupted geometry with a broad garden. Small surviving wedges merge with neighboring rooms. Shared face offsets reserve parallel wall voids; convex decomposition seams inside a single room remain open. The core becomes an enclosed courtyard with sky overhead. The garden reaches the exterior, connects multiple interiors, and has a raised perimeter wall with sky beyond it. Outdoor sky ceilings stand 32 units above the neighboring indoor ceilings, exposing textured wall faces above their openings. The outer horizon wall still hides the map edge. The seed changes the cut depth, direction and optional connections. Connections needed to keep all surviving regions reachable are retained. See [the measured E1M4 structures and construction equations](procedural-structures.md).

Walls are solid voids between paired parallel room faces. Each shared partition line is offset inward on both sides, reserving at least 128 units of perpendicular thickness for recessed doors. Face intersections join those wall strips at corners. Open passages, hallways and doors retain at least 128 units of clear width; curved stairs retain at least 160 units throughout the turn. The footprint and doorway lattice preserve exact integer WAD vertices without independently rounding either wall face. These clusters coexist with rooms fitted to the planar graph. Stable boundary identifiers allow several entrances on one wall.

Ordinary rooms, hallways, lift landings and spiral steps have at least 192 units of headroom. Inter-layer passage roofs allow for the raised 64-unit landings, and adjacent storeys retain a 32-unit slab. The pool is 224 or 240 units deep so the basement fits below the ground layer while its drain height remains representable by a Hexen action argument. Larger ground plans pack into negative WAD X coordinates, separately from the other layers and within the original signed 16-bit coordinate range.

Ordinary room connections and suitable cluster passages each have a **30% chance** of a door. Set `--door-chance=0` for open connections or `--door-chance=100` for doors at every eligible connection. The percentage is rolled independently for each named connection, so increasing it adds doors without reshuffling the room layout. Each hallway connection gets at most one door, on an existing flat landing at either end. Short stair/dogleg landings, lift controls, inter-layer portals and wide merged joins retain their existing geometry. Cluster walls reserve enough depth for lintels; narrow cut remnants merge into neighboring rooms with spacious interiors. Panels use the full 128-unit-wide BIGDOOR2 texture. Diagonal passages whose snapped width exceeds the texture remain open. No scaling is applied; panel height remains 128 units. Closed initial doors do not exclude connected low rooms from the authored flood basin.

A partially rounded hub, at least 1,152 units across, surrounds a 448-unit-wide octagonal chamber with four entrances. A seeded 90-, 180- or 270-degree faceted run is completed by straight gallery bays, giving the hub a distinct non-symmetrical side. The seeded entrance choice adds either open passages or four working doors. Use `--portal-doors=doors` to force doors or `--portal-doors=open` to leave the passages open; the default is `--portal-doors=seeded`. BIGDOOR2 panels are exactly their texture size, 128 × 128 units, regardless of the surrounding room height. Fixed lintels on both sides remain flush with the neighboring walls, with the moving panel recessed 40 units behind each face. Doors open, wait and close, and can be used again from either side. Wide joins share the same floor and ceiling without a dividing wall or portal. Use `--layout=building` to keep all ordinary cores rectangular when packing a building floor; the default is `--layout=organic`. The layout and portal-door option are also recorded in `GENINFO`.

The default output directory is `packages/doom-phonics/demo`. `--output=/tmp/seeded-preview` writes the WAD, browser world JSON and HTML report elsewhere without overwriting the published seed. To serve that world, provide `DOOM1.WAD` beside it and the Doom package manifest at `../phonics.json`, or update those resource paths. The WAD includes its own original water flat and needs no data from `PORTALS.WAD`.

## Generation timing

Every CLI run prints elapsed milliseconds for layout, WAD/BSP construction, runtime validation, recovery attempts, output preparation/writing, and the total. Measurements use PHP's monotonic `hrtime` clock. Total time ends after the three generated assets have been written; it excludes the timing report itself and subsequent site/PHAR builds.

Save a measurement separately for comparisons:

```sh
php packages/doom-phonics/tools/generate-seeded.php --seed=first-light --rooms=14 --timings=/tmp/seeded-timing.json
```

The JSON includes the seed, generator version, layout, region count, WAD size, peak PHP memory and phase timings. Compare identical inputs on the same machine; validation is included, not just geometry emission. Timing data is deliberately excluded from the WAD, world and navigation report so those assets remain reproducible.

## Connectivity and recovery

Each complete candidate is checked before publication. Wall offsets, stair reservations and optional structures receive bounded repairs. A failed detailed candidate retries with simpler geometry, then with level ordinary-room connections. Exhausting the bounded attempts raises an error; an unverified map is never returned. Recovery reasons are recorded, and recovery time is included in the timing report.

The finished WAD is loaded through the game's map parser. Collision sweeps verify passages and portals, including that each witness actually lies in its claimed region. Additional sweeps join the entrance witnesses within each convex region, preventing disconnected obstacle routes from being certified by local crossings alone. Solid pillars are represented by separate surrounding walkable pieces. Actual Use rays and movers verify doors and repeatable lifts. The generator then executes complete player-command walks from spawn to the goal and back in **dry, shallow and full water states**, including swimming, doors and portal falls. A returning player waits outside an existing door cycle before opening it again.

Layer placement uses finished ceilings and maintains the 32-unit slab above occupied ordinary rooms. The upper embedding grows away from the spiral's occupied columns. Selected galleries preserve an open rim and matching recessed apertures across all three levels. An incomplete gallery stack is omitted as a unit. Ordinary optional shafts are reverted if their surrounding walkway does not fit. The flood model retains explicit state heights independently of XY packing. Ordinary basement ceilings are at least as high as the full basin surface, so water cannot project above their portal openings. Ground floors reserve the recessed aperture and a 32-unit slab above those ceilings. The water control line has no wall texture: its liquid flats rise without drawing moving copies of the room walls. Recessed openings include an intermediate 16-unit ledge so a player can keep swimming upward and step onto the dry rim.

The current certification uses the standard 16-unit player radius, 56-unit height, 24-unit maximum step, translation portals, settled water states, and repeatable doors and lifts. Authored gallery drops are checked using actual gravity through intermediate apertures. Sloped catwalks, multiple independent fluids and timed puzzles remain outside this generator's scope.

## Reusable geometry details

`tools/SpiralStaircase.php` builds an annular stair hall with configurable step count, rise, width, 90/180/270-degree sweep, inner radius, mirroring, headroom and minimum inner tread depth. The inner radius grows when necessary to keep the narrow end of every tread usable. The outer radius compensates for faceting and integer rounding so clear width remains at least the requested value. Entrance and exit landings are tangent to the arc and are 256 units long. `tools/CornerStaircase.php` uses the same treads to carve a quarter-turn stair and long raised ledge into a room corner. In SEEDED its ledge serves the higher exit, and the lower entrance remains on the room floor.

`tools/RoomJoin.php` identifies short, same-height rectangular neighbors, adjusts candidate footprints within reserved bays, and replaces their narrow connectors with broad shared floor space. Its area limit bounds the combined size; offset corners remain part of the outline. Architectural and traversal IDs are preserved so other connections and report paths remain valid.

`tools/OutlinePacking.php` unions source polygons before partitioning. `PolygonGeometry::union()` keeps disjoint convex pieces of the exact union, including exterior concavities. `tools/ConformingRooms.php` clips an octagonal core and eight radial partitions to that union before reserving shared walls. It returns convex rooms, the original partitions, and shared wall equations. `RoomIntrusion::partition()` subtracts an invading polygon before those offsets, retaining logical ownership across convex fragments. `RoomCluster::packed()` merges small remnants and selects long enough shared edges for passages or recessed doors. A connectivity forest keeps necessary connections; optional garden connections are seeded. `OutdoorSpace::garden()` adds a bounded sky-wall strip away from reserved entrances and lower ground with return stairs. Custom dimensions still need room-clearance and connectivity validation before publication.

`tools/EmbeddedPlanarGraph.php`, `OrganicPacking.php` and `OrganicLayout.php` separate graph embedding, ownership/cut operations and walkable geometry. `RoomFeatures.php` carves solid obstacles and lowered centers. `PlayerWalk.php` and `RouteTraversal.php` supply the command-level validation shared by generation and test fixtures.

`tools/RoomElevation.php` plans different-height room entrances from their connected passages, reserves flat landings, and subdivides the interior into broad stair strips. The older `RoomRelief.php` carving helper remains available; organic room treatments use `RoomFeatures.php`. `MapConnectivity::rooms()` summarizes internal entrance paths and one-way edges from the physically checked region graph.

`tools/PortalGallery.php` carves a large aperture with an open rim or four outer hallways. A middle gallery carries both floor and ceiling portals. Connectivity validation follows gravity through intermediate apertures and records the final grounded landing, rather than pretending the open shaft is a standing platform.

`tools/PlanePortalSite.php` carves matching square apertures from ordinary convex cores, preserves the surrounding walkways, and recesses the floor side into the slab. The generator pairs these sites with passable floor/ceiling portal controls and records their directed traversal edges. Each pair is checked using actual falling physics, with a return path through the rest of the level.


`tools/DoorRecess.php` splits a clockwise rectangular passage reservation into two fixed lintels and one moving panel, returning both cells and controls for repeatable use from either side. Its first edge is the inner entrance. Cardinal recesses are 40 units deep; diagonal recesses snap to integer vertices while retaining player clearance. Hallway, cluster and portal-well doors share this builder. `DoorRecess::selected()` makes the independent seeded percentage roll.

`tools/SwitchRecess.php` builds an alcove against an axis-aligned exterior wall of a convex room. Supply the wall point and outward normal, panel texture, action/arguments, width, height, sill height, recess depth, and texture crop origin. There is no assumed tile size. `SwitchRecess::panel($textures, $texture)` derives width and height from the full composite texture. Pass `patch: 'SW2S0'` to crop to one named patch at its authored texture offset, or `crop: [$x, $y, $width, $height]` for an explicit rectangle. Unpack the returned width, height and crop into `build()`. Patch mode keeps the composite SW1/SW2 texture on the wall so both switch states animate normally. It returns a decorative sector, a repeatable switch boundary and a control point within Use range. Reserve solid space behind the wall and include the sector in the combined geometry/overlap checks. Shallow panel alcoves are excluded from walkable graph nodes; their controls belong to the adjoining room. The seeded pool uses the same helper four times, with 8-unit recesses. `--switch-panels=patch` (default) uses the 32 × 32 switch patches inside SW1COMP and SW1BRCOM; `--switch-panels=texture` uses their full 64 × 128 and 128 × 128 images respectively. Both choices are recorded in GENINFO and preserve both switch states.

`tools/PolygonMap.php` keeps the horizontal texture offset continuous when splitting a wall around an opening. Set a cell's `alignTextures` to anchor structural upper and lower spans to the ceiling like the surrounding middle wall. `wallXOffset`/`wallYOffset` shift the whole run; boundary `xOffset`/`yOffset` select a panel crop. Boundary `upperUnpegged`/`lowerUnpegged` overrides preserve moving door faces while keeping lintels, risers and door tracks aligned. These are standard Hexen WAD sidedef offsets and pegging flags, with no custom runtime texture scaling.
