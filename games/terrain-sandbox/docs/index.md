# Landscape Sandbox

Landscape Sandbox is a place to explore and edit procedural terrain. The default landscape is about 20 miles on each side. Nearby geometry becomes more detailed as you approach it, while the physical ground remains independent of visual detail.

## Use the Landscape Lab panel

The panel on the right has seven tabs. **Landscape** generates terrain and saves or loads it. **Shape** holds the editing tools, road placement and race gates. **Roads** opens a zoomable terrain map for editing the route, its curves and its elevation profile. **Surface** sets terrain colors, relief and paint inks. **Water** and **Sky** set the water and the sun and atmosphere. **Explore** boards the vehicles and sets their controls. Click a tab, or move between tabs with the arrow keys, **Home** and **End**. The panel opens on the tab you used last. Click the **Landscape Lab** title or press **H** to collapse the panel to a round sliders button. Click the button to reopen it.

The top left of the view lists the controls for what you are doing, drawn as the buttons of the device you used last: the keyboard, an Xbox, PlayStation or other gamepad, or a touchscreen. Switching devices switches the list. The F5's cockpit view hides it behind its own instruments.

On a touchscreen, drag a finger across the view to look around and tap the ground to use the chosen tool. Flying and driving need a gamepad or keyboard.

The soundtrack starts after your first input. The Landscape Lab title bar has **Previous track**, **Play music**, **Pause music** and **Next track**. These work from every tab. Pausing the music lets you keep driving. **Explore** has the music volume slider. The lower-left credit shows the title, artist and available cover art beside the minimap. The player buffers the next song shortly before the current one ends. A race finish briefly crossfades to a jingle, then resumes the song where it paused. Opening the sandbox again starts the next song in the rotation.

On the **Landscape** tab, expand **Landform parameters** to see what each setting changes. The fields sit side by side when there is room and stack on narrow screens. Select **Generate** to apply your changes.

## Color the ground

In **Surface**, open a named material layer to edit its color, coverage or relief. Choose **Load preset surface** to use another preset’s materials on this landform. **Follow landform preset** resumes automatic selection when generating terrain. Paint inks cover the completed stack, so a painted road stays visible when the surrounding ground changes. [Shape a landscape](authoring.md#author-a-surface) includes a sand-ripple example.

**Basalt Fjords** pairs smoky olive heath with blue-black cliffs and dark tidal gravel. **Limestone Plain** has deep green valleys and silver-gray karst. **Mountain Ridges** grades from spruce forest through sage-green meadow to blue-gray granite and snow.

**Arctic Meltwater** opens over a channel between snow-covered shelves. Look along the banks for exposed blue ice and dark moraine gravel. **Tidal Lagoon** has low sand cays with scrub on their crests. Board the watercraft from either opening view. In the lagoon, the clear shallows reveal pale sand and patches of seagrass. **Red Mesas** opens on a red sand plain facing a long flat-topped mesa in low side light, with more buttes on the horizon. Its walls rise 1,115 ft in soft red bands. **Heather Moor** looks down on a peat lochan from a heather ridge. Straw grass fills the hollows, rust bracken patches the steeper slopes and granite shows where the ground steepens most.

**Salt Pans** opens on a pale salt flat with shallow brine pools and a distant rim of dusty foothills. **Cinder Highlands** faces a volcanic cone across a charcoal lava field. Look for lichen and pale ash on the lower ground, then rusty scoria on the upper slopes.

- **Rolling Prairie** opens in tallgrass. Green swales alternate with straw-colored rises, with occasional buttes breaking the horizon.
- **River Valley & Floodplain** starts on a dry bank overlooking a bend. Drive along the meadow and gravel terraces, or board the watercraft to follow the channel.
- **Wooded Foothills** looks across spurs patched with dark woodland canopy. Meadow clearings and exposed rock mark the changes in slope.
- **High Desert Steppe** opens on a sagebrush and gravel apron. Dry washes lead between long benches of rim rock.
- **Alaska Range** looks across a tundra valley toward snowfields broken by dark schist. Follow the low gravel floor to see the mountains from below.
- **Chalk Headlands** overlooks a cove between grass-topped white cliffs. Follow the shore by water to see the flint in the chalk and the shingle at its foot.

## Fly over the landscape

Right-drag on the view to look around. Use **W/A/S/D** to move horizontally and **Q/E** to descend or climb. Hold **Shift** to fly at 671 mph. The Flight slider changes the ordinary movement speed.

With a gamepad, the left stick moves, the right stick looks around, and the right and left triggers climb and descend. Hold the left stick pressed in to fly at 671 mph.

**Home view** returns to the starting viewpoint. **H** folds or opens the controls. On supported browsers the sandbox keeps the screen awake while its tab is visible, including during gamepad play. **Fullscreen** enlarges the whole sandbox, including its controls.

Choose a preset and seed, then select **Generate** to create a new landscape. Generation replaces the current landscape and its edits, so save your work first. A seed reproduces its landscape under the saved generator version.

## Fly the F5

Press **J**, choose **Fly F5**, or press up on a gamepad's D-pad to start airborne at your current location, facing the same direction as the camera. The jet handles like AirForce Delta on the Dreamcast and offers its two control types. Choose one under **Jet controls**, or press **Back** on a gamepad, even in flight.

**Novice**, the default, banks into turns and levels itself. **A/D** or the left stick turn. **E/Q**, or pulling and pushing the stick, climb and dive. Let go and the jet levels its wings and nose.

**Expert** turns the jet about its own axes and holds whatever attitude you leave it in, so it can roll inverted and loop. **A/D** or the left stick rolls the jet. **E/Q** or the left stick controls pitch. **LT/RT** works the rudder left/right, as does **Z/X** or the horizontal right stick. In a bank the wings support only part of the jet's weight unless you pull, so a banked jet turns slowly and one on its side slides toward the ground. Switching back to Novice keeps the jet's orientation and then levels it.

Hold **RT** in Novice or **Y** in Expert to speed up. Hold **LT** in Novice or **B** in Expert to slow down. On the keyboard, **W/S** controls speed in either mode. Airspeed ranges from 123 to 582 mph and returns to 313 mph on release. ENG reads 30% at minimum power, 75% at cruise and 140% at full power. Above 100% the dial warms from green through amber to red as the engine spools up. Novice trigger travel sets the requested power proportionally. Pulling the left stick back raises the nose. **Push the stick forward to climb** reverses pitch. The browser remembers handling and pitch preferences.

Both chase cameras keep the horizon level. The Novice camera looks along the jet's heading. The Expert camera swings in behind the nose, so it follows loops. It turns round quickly as the nose passes straight up or down. **P** or **Start** pauses the flight. Press **F**, or hold **Back** and press **D-pad down**, to leave the jet and explore from the current camera position. Reaching the map edge also returns you to free flight.

### Cockpit and balloon practice

Press **V**, press **Y+B** together, or click the right stick to switch between the chase camera and the pilot’s view. The cockpit looks along the nose and rolls with the wings. The winged diamond marks your direction of travel. Pull up and it drops below center while the jet carries forward. Bank into a turn and it shows how far your flight path trails the nose. It settles back to center in steady, straight flight. The tapes flank the central ring. **SPD** shows speed in mph and **ALT** shows feet above the ground or water.

In both chase and cockpit views, the bottom-left minimap turns with the nose. Straight ahead is always up. The corner label shows the compass heading at the top of the map. Its shaded relief follows the terrain, water level and surface colors. Amber diamonds mark balloons, red identifies the selected one and an edge chevron points toward a balloon outside the map. Press **D-pad down** or **N** to zoom out around the vehicle. The wide view narrows as you approach the boundary so terrain fills the square where possible. Press again to return to the local view.

A green triangle just outside the cockpit ring points toward the nearest balloon within 2.5 miles, including one behind the aircraft. The triangle disappears when that balloon enters the circle. The bottom-right radar uses the same heading. An upward triangle marks a balloon above the aircraft, a downward triangle marks one below and a square marks one at the same altitude. **H** opens the Landscape Lab in flight.

Choose **Balloon practice** under **Explore** and look for the red blip on the minimap. On a full-size landscape the balloon appears 1.1–2.0 miles away, usually 30–75 degrees to either side of your heading. Near the map edge you may need to turn back inland. Follow the blip even if a ridge hides the balloon. The number beside **BAL** shows the straight-line distance in yards. Within about 1,312 yd, hold the selected balloon inside the HUD ring with a clear view. A second diamond moves from the flight-path marker toward the balloon during the 0.3 seconds it takes to lock. It reaches the balloon as the target box turns red. Losing alignment or sight interrupts the approach. **T** or **D-pad up** selects the next balloon.

| Launch a projectile | Space | A |

A projectile follows the balloon selected at launch if you have a lock. Otherwise it travels straight ahead. In chase view, both exhaust nozzles brighten with delivered thrust. Their cores turn bright amber as **ENG** climbs above 100%. You can see one projectile beneath each wing. Launches alternate between loaded wings. Each empty mount refills after three seconds of flight. Projectiles leave a widening smoke trail. On contact with a balloon, ground or water, a flash expands into a fireball and fades to smoke. Tracers have their own firing cadence. A loaded banked turn draws short condensation trails from both wingtips. After a practice balloon pops, the next one favors the opposite side of your current heading. Pause freezes the practice clock.

## Watch the atmosphere

**Sun azimuth** moves the sun around the horizon. **Sun elevation** raises or lowers it. **Haze** adjusts aerosol scattering throughout the atmosphere, and **Exposure** adjusts display brightness. Enable **Animate the sun** to watch the light move.

**Sky light** sets how strongly the sky lights ground the sun misses. The light comes from the sky itself, so it is blue at midday and dimmer and warmer near sunset. **Sky color** picks the blue of the sky overhead with the sun 35° up. A deeper blue also keeps distant land clearer. A paler blue thickens the air and deepens the sunset. The sandbox keeps the sky's colors physically plausible, so an unusual choice lands on the nearest sky it can make. **Haze color** tints the haze. Its effect shows most in the glow around the sun and along the horizon. Each landform starts with its own sky. Mountain Ridges, Canyon, Arctic Meltwater and Tidal Lagoon have deeper blues. Basalt Fjords has a paler northern sky. Canyon and Amber Dunes have dusty haze.

Each landform starts with its own atmosphere. In **Basalt Fjords**, mist softens distant headlands while their tops stay clear. In **Amber Dunes**, suspended sand warms the low horizon. **Arctic Meltwater** has just a trace of ice haze across the water. Open **Sky** and toggle the layer’s **Visible** checkbox to compare the view from the same spot. Fine dust hangs over **Red Mesas**, and a low mist fills the hollows of **Heather Moor**.

To shape the air yourself, edit a layer or choose **Add atmosphere layer**. Set its base and top in feet relative to sea level. Negative elevations place haze down in a canyon. The layer thins toward both boundaries. Choose the color of the scattered light. **Density per mile** controls how quickly haze builds with distance. Looking along a layer shows more color than looking through it from above. Your edits carry over when you generate another landform and are saved with the landscape. **Use preset layers** restores the current landform’s air and follows future preset selections.

As the sun drops below the horizon, stars appear and a blue ambient light picks out the terrain, water, and jet ski. **Night glow** sets the strength of the sky glow and this reflected light. At zero you can see the stars against a dark sky. Set **Sun elevation** to −12° for a quick night preview. The glow setting travels with the saved landscape.

## Adjust the water

**Water level** raises or lowers the surface immediately, in feet. **Choppiness** controls the height and steepness of the waves the jet ski rides. At 0% the surface is calm between wakes, 100% gives the original swell, and 300% triples its height. Both controls work while paused and are included in saved landscapes.

Use **Water color** to tint the surface and underwater haze. Raise **Transparency** to see through the surface. Lower the fog density for a longer view through the water, or raise it for a murkier lake. **Fog density** runs from clear water at the left end to about 0.305 per foot at the right. Its scale is logarithmic, so the low densities of clear lakes get most of the slider's travel. **Reflectivity** controls sky and sun reflections, strongest toward the horizon. **Shore foam** controls the white fringe in the shallows. These settings take effect while riding or paused and travel with a saved landscape.

## Drive on land

Select **Drive car**, press **C**, or press left on a gamepad's D-pad to take the Testarossa. The sandbox checks the ground below you first, then searches nearby for a dry, gently sloping patch with room to pull away. Graded roads use the same ground surface. If you start over water or a cliff and the search finds nothing, fly closer to land and try again.

The round minimap in the lower left keeps the car's heading at the top. Press **M** or click the left stick to show or hide it. Press **N** or tap **D-pad down** to change the zoom. The speedometer in the lower right reads in mph.

Above 40 mph the chase camera moves closer to the road. On level ground its position behind the car is 2.25° lower at 80 mph and 4.5° lower from 120 mph onward. The camera looks about 10° below the horizon while parked and 5.4° below it at high speed. It rises again as you slow down. The vertical field of view opens from 60° while parked to about 70° at 30 mph, 77° at 60 mph and 86° at 120 mph. It reaches 90° at the car's 240 mph top speed.

The automatic gearbox shifts through five forward gears. A light pedal shifts earlier. Full throttle holds each gear longer, and you can hear the revs drop as the next gear engages. Sliding tires squeal on pavement. On dry, unpaved ground, a hard launch, a skid, or nitrous throws dust from the tires touching the ground.

Hold **Shift** or **LB** for nitrous. The lime arc around the speedometer shows the charge. A full bottle gives four seconds of boost. It pulls a skid toward the steered heading and presses the tires into the road over small crests. Release the button and drive forward on the ground to recharge. A quarter mile fills an empty bottle. Boost becomes available again at 3%, about 40 feet after running dry.

**W** or the right trigger accelerates. **S** or the left trigger brakes while moving forward and reverses once the car stops. **A/D** or the left stick steer. The triggers and stick act in proportion to how far you move them. **Space** or **A** pulls the handbrake. The rear tires lock, slow the car and let the tail swing out into a turn. Release it and the car carries on the way it points. On a gamepad the right stick looks around the car; pull it back to look behind. An inward bank helps the tires carry speed through a corner. The wheels move independently over bumps. A crest you can roll over slowly can launch the car at speed. Pitch and roll slow gradually in the air, and the suspension compresses on landing. The tires can hold a steep bank, but enough sideways load or a sharp ramp can still turn the car over. As soon as it lands steadily on its tires, you can steer, accelerate or brake through the remaining slide. If the car comes to rest on its roof, it waits 1.5 seconds before righting itself. It uses the last stable patch if the landing spot is too steep. Shallow water slows the car as its tires sink deeper. If water reaches the engine intake during free driving, the car returns to the last dry, stable spot on its approach. During a race it returns to the course. The race clock runs through the 1.5-second restart. Press **F** to return to flight.

The inside front wheel steers farther than the outside wheel in a turn. The outside springs compress under cornering load and settle as you straighten out. An inward bank supports the turn and reduces body lean. A bank also gently pulls the rolling car toward the lower side with the steering released.

The rear lenses are dark red with the lights off. The running lights cast a soft red glow across the rear grille. Braking brightens both strips and lights the high center lamp. The brakes respond to **S** or the left trigger while the car rolls forward, **W** or the right trigger while it rolls backward, and the handbrake at any speed. The white lamps come on when **S** or the left trigger engages reverse. They stay lit while you coast backward from that maneuver or hold reverse at a standstill. While the car tumbles or lies on its roof, the tail lamps glow brightly only while you hold **S**, the left trigger or the handbrake, and the white lamps stay dark.

Press **L**, press **X** on a gamepad, or choose **Car headlights** in the panel to switch the headlights between **Auto**, **On** and **Off**. Auto lights them while the sun is below 5 degrees, and by day wherever a hill or canyon wall hides the sun from the car. It turns them off again after two seconds back in the sun, so driving along the edge of a shadow does not flicker them. When the headlights come on, the pop-up headlights open over 1.2 seconds, the front lamps glow and the tail lamps glow dimly; braking brightens them. Once the pop-up headlights rise clear of the nose, two beams light the road, the ground and the water ahead of the car; on level ground they dim with distance and fade out about 295 ft ahead. By day the beams barely show in direct sun. In a shadow, as at night, your eyes adjust to the dark over about a second, and the beams light the ground about 100 to 130 feet ahead. The deeper the shadow compared with the sunlit ground, the brighter they look. The beams pass through hills and light neither the car nor the race gates. Water shows a faint glow from them but no reflection. The headlights keep to their switch while the car tumbles or lies on its roof, and the beams point wherever the nose points. While the game is paused, changing the switch, or moving the sun past 5 degrees with the switch on Auto, opens or closes the pop-up headlights at once. Leaving the car puts the beams out. The sandbox remembers the headlight switch in this browser. Saved landscapes do not carry it.

## Run packages

Select **Explore → Package run** to take the car. The red depot stays where the run starts. If you start on a canyon mesa, the game boards the car on a nearby dry valley floor first. Follow the lime arrow to a box beneath yellow-green smoke and drive through it to collect it. The box stops emitting smoke when collected. The puffs already in the air rise and disperse over the next nine seconds, and the arrow turns red for the return trip. Drive between the four depot posts to deliver the box and start the next pickup. You can also jump over the box or depot and score up to 26.2 ft above the site's ground level. The minimap keeps the depot marked with a red ring throughout the run and shows the current box as a lime square. Markers at its edge show which way to drive to a site beyond the map.

Every pickup must be at least one mile from the depot in a straight line. If the reachable ground offers no hidden site that far away, the run waits for a valid site. In a full-size canyon, the search looks 2.6–3.5 miles from the depot for a valley floor on the far side of a plateau. It checks that a broad stretch of rock hides the entire smoke column. Successive trips favor different branches and give the last three pickups room. In confined valleys or lower terrain, smaller ridges can hide the crate while the smoke rises above them.

Pickups need a dry, car-width route to the depot. The search tries 630 ft links, then retries at 315 ft to catch distant bends and at a finer scale near the depot. Where the height bounds establish a dry, gentle crossing, the search can skip detailed sampling. Elsewhere it checks both tire tracks and the center every 6.6 ft. If there is no reachable cover, drive to another position to search again. Editing the terrain or changing the water level triggers a fresh route check.

The smoke rises about 361 ft. The HUD shows yards to the pickup or depot and keeps a delivery count until you choose **End package run** or leave the car. Ending the run restores checkpoint navigation on a course.

## Ride the water

Select **Watercraft**, press **B**, or press right on a gamepad's D-pad to find nearby water deep enough for the craft. Use **W/S** or the triggers for throttle and reverse, and **A/D** or the left stick to steer. The right stick looks around the craft. The speedometer in the lower right shows your speed in mph. Hold full throttle on open water to pass 90 mph. The craft responds to the same large waves shown by the renderer and leaves local wake disturbances.

Select **Return to flight**, or press **F**, to explore and edit again. The craft is an arcade physics demonstration, not a complete racing game.

## Race through gates

Choose **Race gate** under **Tool** to build a course in free flight. Click the ground to place a gate. It faces the way the camera looks, so you pass through it traveling that way. Where you click decides the kind of gate:

- On water, an inflatable arch for the jet ski, 29.2 ft wide and 11.8 ft high inside. It floats and rises and falls with the waves.
- On land with **Height above ground** at 0, a gantry on two solid posts for the car, 43.6 ft wide and 10.2 ft high inside. The car collides with the posts. On a slope the gantry stands on the higher ground across its span, so the board stays clear of the hill; each post reaches down into the ground under it, and the opening reaches down to the lower side. Shaping the ground under a gate afterward fits the gate to it again.
- On land with a height above 0, a banner for the F5 that hangs that many feet above the ground. Fly through the space below it, 131 ft wide and 28.9 ft high. The F5 starts at least 246 ft above the ground, so a height of 233 ft puts the opening in its path.

**Opening width** and **Opening height** size the next gate you place; 0 keeps the kind's own size. Road and water gates open up to 197 ft wide and 131 ft high, and air gates up to 984 by 492 feet. The road gate’s posts move to the sides of the opening. The board above them centers the lettering between two checker bands. On narrow gates the letters shrink proportionally so the checks remain visible. The arch stretches, at most twice as much one way as the other, so a wide arch must also be tall. A size beyond a kind's range takes the nearest one it allows, and the message says so. The note under the two fields lists each kind's range and own size.

The F5 keeps at least 26.2 ft above the ground and the water, so it flies under a road gate's board only when the opening is at least 29.5 ft high. When the vehicle you are in cannot reach the next gate's opening, such as the F5 at a road gate of its own size, the car at a gate on the water, or the jet ski at a gate on land, the race line says so, and for the F5 gives the opening height it needs. The car passes through an air gate whose opening starts up to 1.6 feet above the ground, and the jet ski one up to 9.8 ft above the water.

**Gate role** chooses what a click does. A course has one start gate and one finish gate, and placing another moves it. A finish gate placed within one gate width of the start gate is not added; the start gate becomes a start / finish gate instead. Checkpoints are numbered in the order you place them, up to 32. A gate that would overlap another gate is refused. **Remove** takes away the gate you click on or beside, and the checkpoints after it move up a number. **Clear gates** removes every gate.

**Laps** belongs to the start gate, runs from 1 to 20, and counts only on a course without a finish gate. Each lap then passes the checkpoints in order and returns through the start gate, which reads START / FINISH. A course with a finish gate runs once, from the start gate through the checkpoints to the finish gate. The note under **Laps** says which of the two the course you have built does.

Drive, ride or fly through the start gate to start the clock; you do not need to stop first. Pass each gate in order and in its direction. In the car, jumping over a road gate counts when you cross between its posts. The race ignores every other crossing. A course without a finish gate ends each lap back at the start gate, and the lap counts only once you have been at least three half widths of that gate's frame away from it since you last passed through it: 75.5 ft from a road gate, 59.1 ft from a water gate or 223 ft from an air gate at their own sizes. Rocking back and forth through the start gate, or through it and a checkpoint just clear of it, counts no lap. The line under the status message shows the time, the lap, the next gate and its distance in yards, the last split and the best time. The sandbox keeps your best time for each course until you reload the page. **Back + Y**, **R**, or **Reset race** returns the clock to the start gate. In the car or watercraft, **Y** alone resets a finished race. Pausing stops the clock. Leaving the vehicle, boarding again, reaching the map edge or changing the course stops the race.

The chase camera never passes through a gate. Behind a vehicle that goes through the opening, it lowers itself under the board, banner or arch and rises again once past, keeping clear of the posts and legs, at whatever size the gate is. The car's camera starts lowering 32.8 ft before a gate, the jet ski's 26.2 ft and the F5's 197 ft. Behind a vehicle that goes over a gate, the camera passes over it too, and when the F5 climbs away before it reaches a gate, as it does looping up in front of one, the camera lets that gate go and follows the jet up. When the look stick swings the camera into a post, the camera stays on its side of the post, or goes round the post's face when the stick swings it farther across.

**Save file** stores the gates and their sizes with the landscape, and **Load file** brings them back. Gates in files saved before gates had sizes load at their kinds' own sizes. **Generate** starts the new landscape without gates.

The chase camera keeps at least 1.6 feet above the road while it dodges a gate, including on banks and crests.

The overhead arrow shows its underside. Straight ahead points down the screen, a U-turn points up, and the end nearest you appears larger. During a race it turns amber and points to the nearest point along the next opening. It holds its direction within 4.9 ft and turns smoothly toward the following gate. The minimap marks the gate’s center. Crossing the finish clears the arrow, checkpoint marker and route overlay. Your final time pulses three times, then stays steady until the readout clears four seconds after the finish. With reduced motion enabled, the time stays steady throughout. Crossing the start again brings them back.

During a road race, click the right stick if you leave the road. Right-stick recovery and a flooded engine both return the car to a dry, safe point on the current leg, facing forward with at least 32.8 ft of road before the next checkpoint. If that leg is submerged, recovery works back from the last gate you passed. On a course built with gates alone, a flooded car restarts on a cleared gate’s approach. You keep your splits, and the clock runs through the 1.5-second restart.

Yellow chevrons mark paved bends that turn at least 30° with a radius of 300 ft or less. Their faces measure about 13 by 16 ft. The signs stand about six yards apart along the outside shoulder and point into the bend from either direction. At night the faces and the printed checkpoint banners reflect the car’s headlights back toward the driver. You can drive straight through the signs and their posts.

### Generate a circuit

In free flight, open **Roads → Circuit generator**. Set **Minimum feature height difference** to a positive value to circle high ground or a negative value to circle a lake below the road. For example, **+656 ft** in the default Canyon looks for a feature whose high point rises at least 656 ft above the road. **−197 ft** in the default Mountain Ridges looks for a road at least 197 ft above a lake at the current water level.

Leave **Start elevation** blank to put the start near the camera, or enter an absolute terrain elevation to put it on a straight within 1.6 feet of that height. This sets the height of the start station. The rest of the road follows the route's natural elevation. Select **Find circuit near camera** to preview a match. Large height differences favor a regional search that follows connected corridors and places waypoints after junctions where possible.

To cross water, turn on **Allow causeways** before finding the circuit. The search favors dry ground and can raise a paved embankment over water up to 98.4 ft deep. It checks both road edges against the highest configured waves. In **Arctic Meltwater**, keep the preset water level, leave **Start elevation** blank, and choose **Apply dry course** to drive the raised crossings.

The preview shows the route and gate positions. The road picker groups the fitted curves by circuit section. To adjust part of a bend, choose **Place points** and click the curve to add a control point. To lay out your own course, place three or more points in **Roads** and choose **Close loop and create circuit**. A regional course grades a 118 ft road on application. It rounds off short crests and eases the grade through tight bends while carrying natural banks into the turns. The shoulders blend into the surrounding terrain. The road is one terrain edit that **Undo** can remove. Choose **Apply dry course** for the car, or **Apply flooded course** if the loop can hold enough water for the jet ski. On a paved dry circuit, the start/finish and checkpoint posts sit 2.5 ft outside each edge of the pavement. The vertical clearance is 20 ft. Both variants use the same route and checkpoint order. Applying a variant replaces the current gates, sets its water level and moves the camera just before the start. Board the vehicle there and follow the line on the minimap. The amber blip marks the next gate.

Generated and hand-drawn circuits have up to 11 physical gates per lap, including start/finish, with at least 150 ft of road between them. Drive through the numbered gates in order to record splits. Between gates, the arrow guides you through unmarked openings. Each spans the pavement or the width of a water arch. The arrow points to the closest point across the opening, so you can stay in your lane as you pass.

Longer laps get more waypoints per mile, down to a target spacing of 200 ft. Waypoints always leave at least 100 ft to the next waypoint or gate, including across the lap line. If you leave the racing line, the arrow picks up the route near you when you rejoin.

Landscape files save the applied variant and both available water levels, so you can switch variants after loading. Run the search again after changing the terrain.

## Use a gamepad

The sandbox reads gamepads that the browser maps to the standard layout, such as Xbox and PlayStation controllers. Browsers report a pad only after you press one of its buttons with the page open. Outside the F5, the D-pad chooses your vehicle: up for the jet, left for the car, right for the watercraft and down for free flight. **Start** pauses, **Back + Y** resets a race and **X** switches the car headlights or fires the F5’s Vulcan. The gamepad keeps working while a panel control has focus, and stops while the page is in the background.

## Pause and save

**P** pauses waves, the moving sun, and vehicle motion. Camera movement and authoring remain available. Once terrain preparation settles, an unchanged paused view stops submitting rendered frames.

**Save file** downloads a JSON document containing the landscape recipe, committed edits, environment controls, camera and race gates. **Load file** opens a file picker. Files are local to your device; the sandbox does not upload them.

## Choose detail

Economical, Balanced, and Detailed adjust terrain mesh density. They do not change collision heights or saved terrain. Check **Show diagnostics** under **Explore** to add renderer statistics below the status line: rendered patches, mesh memory, generation time, and render submission time. This browser remembers the choice. Render submission measures how long the application spends issuing the scene and presenting it. The interval between draws includes the rest of the frame and any idle time. During continuous animation, 16.7 ms between draws corresponds to 60 frames per second.

The sandbox gives terrain generation an eight-millisecond work budget while the view is stationary and reduces it to two milliseconds while flying, looking, or driving. Work stops when the requested terrain is ready; this budget does not make settled paused views redraw.

The first target is a desktop with hardware-accelerated WebGL2. Terrain and shaders also run with software WebGL, but comparable frame rates are not expected.
