# Landscape Sandbox

Landscape Sandbox is a place to explore and edit procedural terrain. The default landscape is about 32 kilometres square. Nearby geometry becomes more detailed as you approach it, while the physical ground remains independent of visual detail.

## Use the Landscape Lab panel

The panel on the right has six tabs. **Landscape** generates terrain and saves or loads it. **Shape** holds the editing tools, road grading and race gates. **Surface** colours the ground. **Water** and **Sky** set the water and the sun and atmosphere. **Explore** boards the vehicles and sets their controls. Click a tab, or move between tabs with the arrow keys, **Home** and **End**. The panel opens on the tab you used last. **H** folds or opens the whole panel.

## Colour the ground

The **Surface** tab sets the colours of the generated ground, the rock bands on coasts and early canyons, and where one material gives way to another: rock on steep slopes, a beach above the water, snow above the snowline and, on mountains, forest, meadow and tundra below it. Each preset reads its own settings, so the tab shows only those for the current landscape. Mountains show granite, talus and stain, and canyons show their rock beds. A blend's start stays below its end: moving one past the other moves both. **Reset** returns one group to its defaults, and **Reset the whole surface** returns them all. The surface settings travel with a saved landscape and stay when you generate a new one.

## Fly over the landscape

Right-drag on the view to look around. Use **W/A/S/D** to move horizontally and **Q/E** to descend or climb. Hold **Shift** to fly at 300 metres per second. The Flight slider changes the ordinary movement speed.

With a gamepad, the left stick moves, the right stick looks around, and the right and left triggers climb and descend. Hold the left stick pressed in to fly at 300 metres per second.

**Home view** returns to the starting viewpoint. **H** folds or opens the controls. **Fullscreen** enlarges the whole sandbox, including its controls.

Choose a preset and seed, then select **Generate** to create a new landscape. Generation replaces the current landscape and its edits, so save your work first. A seed reproduces its landscape under the saved generator version.

## Fly the F5

Press **J**, choose **Fly F5**, or press up on a gamepad's D-pad to start airborne at your current location, facing the same direction as the camera. The jet handles like AirForce Delta on the Dreamcast and offers its two control types. Choose one under **Jet controls**, or press **Back** on a gamepad, even in flight.

**Novice**, the default, banks into turns and levels itself. **A/D** or the left stick turn. **E/Q**, or pulling and pushing the stick, climb and dive. Let go and the jet levels its wings and nose.

**Expert** turns the jet about its own axes and holds whatever attitude you leave it in, so it can roll inverted and loop. **A/D** or the left stick rolls the jet. **E/Q** or the left stick controls pitch. **LT/RT** works the rudder left/right, as does **Z/X** or the horizontal right stick. In a bank the wings support only part of the jet's weight unless you pull, so a banked jet turns slowly and one on its side slides towards the ground. Switching back to Novice keeps the jet's orientation and then levels it.

Hold **RT** in Novice or **Y** in Expert to speed up. Hold **LT** in Novice or **B** in Expert to slow down. On the keyboard, **W/S** controls speed in either mode. Airspeed ranges from 55 to 260 metres per second and returns to 140 on release. ENG reads 30% at minimum power, 75% at cruise and 140% at full power. Above 100% the dial warms from green through amber to red as the engine spools up. Novice trigger travel sets the requested power proportionally. Pulling the left stick back raises the nose. **Push the stick forward to climb** reverses pitch. The browser remembers handling and pitch preferences.

Both chase cameras keep the horizon level. The Novice camera looks along the jet's heading. The Expert camera swings in behind the nose, so it follows loops. It turns round quickly as the nose passes straight up or down. **P** or **Start** pauses the flight. Press **F**, or hold **Back** and press **D-pad down**, to leave the jet and explore from the current camera position. Reaching the map edge also returns you to free flight.

### Cockpit and balloon practice

Press **V**, press **Y+B** together, or click the right stick to switch between the chase camera and the pilot’s view. The cockpit looks along the nose and rolls with the wings. The winged diamond marks your direction of travel. Pull up and it drops below centre while the jet carries forward. Bank into a turn and it shows how far your flight path trails the nose. It settles back to centre in steady, straight flight. The tapes flank the central ring. **SPD** shows speed in km/h and **ALT** shows metres above the ground or water.

In both chase and cockpit views, the bottom-left minimap turns with the nose. Straight ahead is always up. The corner label shows the compass heading at the top of the map. Its shaded relief follows the terrain, water level and surface colours. Amber diamonds mark balloons, red identifies the selected one and an edge chevron points towards a balloon outside the map. Press **D-pad down** or **N** to zoom out around the vehicle. The wide view narrows as you approach the boundary so terrain fills the square where possible. Press again to return to the local view.

A green triangle just outside the cockpit ring points toward the nearest balloon within 4 km, including one behind the aircraft. The triangle disappears when that balloon enters the circle. The bottom-right radar uses the same heading. An upward triangle marks a balloon above the aircraft, a downward triangle marks one below and a square marks one at the same altitude. **H** opens the Landscape Lab in flight.

Choose **Balloon practice** under **Explore** and look for the red blip on the minimap. On a full-size landscape the balloon appears 1.8–3.2 km away, usually 30–75 degrees to either side of your heading. Near the map edge you may need to turn back inland. Follow the blip even if a ridge hides the balloon. Within 1,200 metres, hold the selected balloon inside the HUD ring with a clear view. A second diamond moves from the flight-path marker toward the balloon during the 0.3 seconds it takes to lock. It reaches the balloon as the target box turns red. Losing alignment or sight interrupts the approach. **T** or **D-pad up** selects the next balloon.

A projectile follows the balloon selected at launch if you have a lock. Otherwise it travels straight ahead. In chase view, both exhaust nozzles brighten with delivered thrust. Their cores turn bright amber as **ENG** climbs above 100%. You can see one projectile beneath each wing. Launches alternate between loaded wings. Each empty mount refills after three seconds of flight. Projectiles leave a widening smoke trail. On contact with a balloon, ground or water, a flash expands into a fireball and fades to smoke. Tracers have their own firing cadence. A loaded banked turn draws short condensation trails from both wingtips. After a practice balloon pops, the next one favours the opposite side of your current heading. Pause freezes the practice clock.

## Watch the atmosphere

Sun azimuth moves the sun around the horizon; elevation raises or lowers it. Haze controls aerosols, and Exposure adjusts the displayed brightness. Enable **Animate the sun** to see the light move over time.

As the sun drops below the horizon, stars appear and a blue ambient light picks out the terrain, water, and jet ski. **Night glow** sets the strength of the sky glow and this reflected light. At zero you can see the stars against a dark sky. Set **Sun elevation** to −12° for a quick night preview. The glow setting travels with the saved landscape.

## Adjust the water

**Water level** raises or lowers the surface immediately, in metres. **Choppiness** controls the height and steepness of the waves the jet ski rides. At 0% the surface is calm between wakes, 100% gives the original swell, and 300% triples its height. Both controls work while paused and are included in saved landscapes.

Use **Water color** to tint the surface and underwater haze. Raise **Transparency** to see through the surface. Lower the fog density for a longer view through the water, or raise it for a murkier lake. **Fog density · coarse** covers the full range from 0 to 1 per metre. **Fog density · fine** adjusts within 0.005 of the coarse setting in increments of 0.0001 per metre. **Reflectivity** controls sky and sun reflections, strongest toward the horizon. **Shore foam** controls the white fringe in the shallows. These settings take effect while riding or paused and travel with a saved landscape.

## Drive on land

Select **Drive car**, press **C**, or press left on a gamepad's D-pad to take the Testarossa. The sandbox checks the ground below you first, then searches nearby for a dry, gently sloping patch with room to pull away. Graded roads use the same ground surface. If you start over water or a cliff and the search finds nothing, fly closer to land and try again.

The minimap in the lower left keeps the car's heading at the top. Press **M** or click the left stick to show or hide it. Press **N** or tap **D-pad down** to change the zoom. The speedometer in the lower right reads in mph. The car can reach 240 mph on a long, level straight.

**W** or the right trigger accelerates. **S** or the left trigger brakes while moving forward and reverses once the car stops. **A/D** or the left stick steer. The triggers and stick act in proportion to how far you move them. **Space** or **A** pulls the handbrake. The rear tires lock, slow the car and let the tail swing out into a turn. Release it and the car carries on the way it points. On a gamepad the right stick looks around the car; pull it back to look behind. An inward bank helps the tires carry speed through a corner. The wheels move independently over bumps. A crest you can roll over slowly can launch the car at speed. It carries its momentum through the air, and the suspension compresses on landing. Steeper faces can tip the car into a roll. As soon as it lands steadily on its tires, you can steer, accelerate or brake through the remaining slide. If the car comes to rest on its roof, it waits 1.5 seconds before righting itself. It uses the last stable patch if the landing spot is too steep. Water and the map edge still bound the drive. Press **F** to return to flight.

The inside front wheel steers farther than the outside wheel in a turn. A bank gently pulls the rolling car toward the lower side, even with the steering released.

The red tail lamps glow brightly while you brake: **S** or the left trigger while the car rolls forward, **W** or the right trigger while it rolls backwards, and the handbrake at any speed. Coasting leaves them dark. The white lamps above them light while the car rolls backwards or you hold reverse at a standstill. While the car tumbles or lies on its roof, the tail lamps glow brightly only while you hold **S**, the left trigger or the handbrake, and the white lamps stay dark.

Press **L**, press **X** on a gamepad, or choose **Car headlights** in the panel to switch the headlights between **Auto**, **On** and **Off**. Auto lights them while the sun is below 5 degrees. When the headlights come on, the pop-up headlights open over 1.2 seconds, the front lamps glow and the tail lamps glow dimly; braking brightens them. Once the pop-up headlights rise clear of the nose, two beams light the road, the ground and the water ahead of the car; on level ground they dim with distance and fade out about 90 metres ahead. The beams shine only once the sun has set. Before then, On and Auto light the lamps and open the pop-up headlights without beams. The beams pass through hills and light neither the car nor the race gates. Water shows a faint glow from them but no reflection. The headlights keep to their switch while the car tumbles or lies on its roof, and the beams point wherever the nose points. While the game is paused, changing the switch, or moving the sun past 5 degrees with the switch on Auto, opens or closes the pop-up headlights at once. Leaving the car puts the beams out. The sandbox remembers the headlight switch in this browser. Saved landscapes do not carry it.

## Run packages

Select **Explore → Package run** to take the car. The red depot stays where the run starts. If you start on a canyon mesa, the game boards the car on a nearby dry valley floor first. Follow the lime arrow to a box beneath yellow-green smoke and drive through it to collect it. The arrow turns red for the return trip. Drive between the four depot posts to deliver the box and start the next pickup. The minimap keeps the depot marked with a red ring throughout the run and shows the current box as a lime square. Markers at its edge show which way to drive to a site beyond the map.

On a full-size landscape the search starts 2.4–3.5 miles from the car, outside your view. Pickups use ground connected to the depot by a dry, car-width route. In a canyon the box goes on the valley floor, with preference for a drive around high ground. In a confined valley or on a small island, the search follows drivable ground outward from the depot to find a closer box. Later deliveries reuse checked routes until you edit the terrain or change the water level. The smoke rises about 110 metres and terrain can hide it. The run keeps a delivery count until you choose **End package run** or leave the car. Ending the run restores checkpoint navigation on a course.

## Ride the water

Select **Board watercraft**, press **B**, or press right on a gamepad's D-pad to find nearby water deep enough for the craft. Use **W/S** or the triggers for throttle and reverse, and **A/D** or the left stick to steer. The right stick looks around the craft. The speedometer in the lower right shows your speed in mph. Hold full throttle on open water to pass 90 mph. The craft responds to the same large waves shown by the renderer and leaves local wake disturbances.

Select **Return to flight**, or press **F**, to explore and edit again. The craft is an arcade physics demonstration, not a complete racing game.

## Race through gates

Choose **Race gate** under **Tool** to build a course in free flight. Click the ground to place a gate. It faces the way the camera looks, so you pass through it travelling that way. Where you click decides the kind of gate:

- On water, an inflatable arch for the jet ski, 8.9 metres wide and 3.6 metres high inside. It floats and rises and falls with the waves.
- On land with **Height above ground** at 0, a gantry on two solid posts for the car, 13.3 metres wide and 3.1 metres high inside. The car collides with the posts. On a slope the gantry stands on the higher ground across its span, so the board stays clear of the hill; each post reaches down into the ground under it, and the opening reaches down to the lower side. Shaping the ground under a gate afterwards fits the gate to it again.
- On land with a height above 0, a banner for the F5 that hangs that many metres above the ground. Fly through the space below it, 40 metres wide and 8.8 metres high. The F5 starts at least 75 metres above the ground, so a height of 71 metres puts the opening in its path.

**Opening width** and **Opening height** size the next gate you place; 0 keeps the kind's own size. Road and water gates open up to 60 metres wide and 40 metres high, and air gates up to 300 by 150 metres. The road gate's posts move to the sides of the opening and its board sits on top, showing as many checker squares as fill it beside an unchanged label. The arch stretches, at most twice as much one way as the other, so a wide arch must also be tall. A size beyond a kind's range takes the nearest one it allows, and the message says so. The note under the two fields lists each kind's range and own size.

The F5 keeps at least 8 metres above the ground and the water, so it flies under a road gate's board only when the opening is at least 9 metres high. When the vehicle you are in cannot reach the next gate's opening, such as the F5 at a road gate of its own size, the car at a gate on the water, or the jet ski at a gate on land, the race line says so, and for the F5 gives the opening height it needs. The car passes through an air gate whose opening starts up to half a metre above the ground, and the jet ski one up to 3 metres above the water.

**Gate role** chooses what a click does. A course has one start gate and one finish gate, and placing another moves it. A finish gate placed within one gate width of the start gate is not added; the start gate becomes a start / finish gate instead. Checkpoints are numbered in the order you place them, up to 32. A gate that would overlap another gate is refused. **Remove** takes away the gate you click on or beside, and the checkpoints after it move up a number. **Clear gates** removes every gate.

**Laps** belongs to the start gate, runs from 1 to 20, and counts only on a course without a finish gate. Each lap then passes the checkpoints in order and returns through the start gate, which reads START / FINISH. A course with a finish gate runs once, from the start gate through the checkpoints to the finish gate. The note under **Laps** says which of the two the course you have built does.

Drive, ride or fly through the start gate to start the clock; you do not need to stop first. Pass each gate in order and in its direction. In the car, jumping over a road gate counts when you cross between its posts. The race ignores every other crossing. A course without a finish gate ends each lap back at the start gate, and the lap counts only once you have been at least three half widths of that gate's frame away from it since you last passed through it: 23 m from a road gate, 18 m from a water gate or 68 m from an air gate at their own sizes. Rocking back and forth through the start gate, or through it and a checkpoint just clear of it, counts no lap. The line under the status message shows the time, the lap, the next gate and its distance, the last split and the best time. The sandbox keeps your best time for each course until you reload the page. **R**, **Reset race** or **Y** on a gamepad (**Back + Y** in the F5) sends the race back to wait at the start gate. Pausing stops the clock. Leaving the vehicle, boarding again, reaching the map edge or changing the course stops the race.

The chase camera never passes through a gate. Behind a vehicle that goes through the opening, it lowers itself under the board, banner or arch and rises again once past, keeping clear of the posts and legs, at whatever size the gate is. The car's camera starts lowering 10 metres before a gate, the jet ski's 8 metres and the F5's 60 metres. Behind a vehicle that goes over a gate, the camera passes over it too, and when the F5 climbs away before it reaches a gate, as it does looping up in front of one, the camera lets that gate go and follows the jet up. When the look stick swings the camera into a post, the camera stays on its side of the post, or goes round the post's face when the stick swings it farther across.

**Save file** stores the gates and their sizes with the landscape, and **Load file** brings them back. Gates in files saved before gates had sizes load at their kinds' own sizes. **Generate** starts the new landscape without gates.

The overhead arrow shows its underside. Straight ahead points down the screen, a U-turn points up, and the end nearest you appears larger. During a race it turns amber and points toward the next gate, starting with the start gate. The minimap shows the same target.

### Generate a circuit

In free flight, open **Shape → Circuit generator**. Set **Minimum feature height difference** to a positive value to circle high ground or a negative value to circle a lake below the road. For example, **+200 m** in the default Canyon country looks for a feature whose high point rises at least 200 m above the road. **−60 m** in the default Mountain Ridges looks for a road at least 60 m above a lake at the current water level.

Leave **Start elevation** blank to put the start near the camera, or enter an absolute terrain elevation to put it on a straight within half a metre of that height. This sets the height of the start station. The rest of the road follows the route's natural elevation. Select **Find circuit near camera** to preview a match. Large height differences favour a regional search that follows connected corridors and places checkpoints after junctions where possible.

The preview shows the route and gate positions. A regional course grades a 36 m road on application. It rounds off short crests and eases the grade through tight bends while carrying natural banks into the turns. The shoulders blend into the surrounding terrain. The road is one terrain edit that **Undo** can remove. Choose **Apply dry course** for the car, or **Apply flooded course** if the loop can hold enough water for the jet ski. The dry circuit has 6.1 metres of clearance under each gantry, including the start/finish line. Both variants use the same route and checkpoint order. Applying a variant replaces the current gates, sets its water level and moves the camera just before the start. Board the vehicle there and follow the line on the minimap. The amber blip marks the next gate.

Landscape files save the applied variant and both available water levels, so you can switch variants after loading. Run the search again after changing the terrain.

## Use a gamepad

The sandbox reads gamepads that the browser maps to the standard layout, such as Xbox and PlayStation controllers. Browsers report a pad only after you press one of its buttons with the page open. Outside the F5, the D-pad chooses your vehicle: up for the jet, left for the car, right for the watercraft and down for free flight. **Start** pauses, **Y** resets a race outside the F5 (**Back + Y** in flight) and **X** switches the car headlights or fires the F5’s Vulcan. The gamepad keeps working while a panel control has focus, and stops while the page is in the background.

## Pause and save

**P** pauses waves, the moving sun, and vehicle motion. Camera movement and authoring remain available. Once terrain preparation settles, an unchanged paused view stops submitting rendered frames.

**Save file** downloads a JSON document containing the landscape recipe, committed edits, environment controls, camera and race gates. **Load file** opens a file picker. Files are local to your device; the sandbox does not upload them.

## Choose detail

Economical, Balanced, and Detailed adjust terrain mesh density. They do not change collision heights or saved terrain. The diagnostic display reports rendered patches, mesh memory, generation time, and render submission time. Render submission measures how long the application spends issuing the scene and presenting it. The interval between draws includes the rest of the frame and any idle time. During continuous animation, 16.7 ms between draws corresponds to 60 frames per second.

The sandbox gives terrain generation an eight-millisecond work budget while the view is stationary and reduces it to two milliseconds while flying, looking, or driving. Work stops when the requested terrain is ready; this budget does not make settled paused views redraw.

The first target is a desktop with hardware-accelerated WebGL2. Terrain and shaders also run with software WebGL, but comparable frame rates are not expected.
