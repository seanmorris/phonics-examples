# Landscape Sandbox

An offline authoring and flight sandbox for the optional `terrain-phonics` package. Fly over procedural mountains, canyons, or coasts; shape the ground; grade roads; adjust the sun and atmosphere; drive a car, ride a buoyant watercraft, or fly an F5, and race each of them through gates you place.

See [the controls guide](docs/index.md) and [landscape authoring](docs/authoring.md).

The terrain, camera, editing, and simulation run in PHP. Browser code provides accessible authoring controls, file dialogs, input collection, and lifecycle integration. The example does not require Tiled or a network connection.

Run `node scripts/bench-terrain.mjs` from the examples checkout with the preview running to measure cold and warm road clicks and flight frame intervals. The report includes the GPU, drawing-buffer size, time to each confirmed marker, and time spent in the picker. Set `TERRAIN_URL` for another preview or `VIEW_WIDTH` and `VIEW_HEIGHT` for a different browser viewport.

While the view moves, the renderer selects terrain detail at most ten times a second. To compare frame rates without that limit, run `document.querySelector('canvas').terrainSandboxDebug.values({selectionInterval: 0})` in the browser console, or pass another spacing in seconds up to 1. The same object's `state.selectionInterval` reports the value in use and `state.renderer.selections` counts selections. The value has no control in the panel and is not saved with a landscape.

A selection that needs more than 3 milliseconds of work continues over the following frames and changes the drawn terrain in one of them. Set `selectionSliceMs` through the same `values()` call to give each frame more or less of that work, or to 0 to run every selection whole in one frame as before. `state.selectionSliceMs` reports the value in use, and `state.renderer` reports `selectionWorkMs` for the last frame, `selectionUpdates` for the frames the last selection took and `selectionInFlight` while one is under way. This value is not saved either.

The status object reports the race gates in `state.course` and the race in `state.race`. `state.renderer.gateDraws` counts the gates the last frame drew, after culling by distance and by the view, and `state.renderer.gateMs` is the time it spent on them in milliseconds.

The Testarossa's brake and reversing lamps follow the pedals and the handbrake, and its tail lamps light with the headlights. **L** and a gamepad's **X** step the headlights through Auto, On and Off, and the **Car headlights** select sets them; the Drive on land section of [the controls guide](docs/index.md) gives the rules. While the car is driven with its headlights lit, `VehicleModel::spotLights()` builds one light per headlamp from the pose each frame draws, and `application.php` passes them to the terrain package's `Scene::setSpotLights()` whenever they change. In every other case the application clears them. `VehicleModel::BEAM_INTENSITY`, `BEAM_RANGE`, `BEAM_INNER` and `BEAM_OUTER` set each beam's intensity, range and cone; the intensity scales with how far the headlamps have lit, and stays at zero until the rising pods lift the headlamps clear of the nose (`BEAM_CLEAR`, which `tests/lamps.php` checks against the mesh). `state.headlights` reports the switch and `state.headlightsLit` whether it lights them now. While driving, `state.lamps` gives each lamp's level and the pods' opening. `state.renderer.spotLights` counts the lights set.

## Watercraft model

The watercraft uses “Jetski” by Futurealiti. The sandbox uses a simplified mesh and a resized dashboard texture. To regenerate them, run `python3 scripts/import-terrain-jetski.py /path/to/jetski.zip` with gltfpack 0.18 and ImageMagick on the path. [Original model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/jetski-15e79c65f67a468592cdc2ac1f5b389b) · [Author](https://sketchfab.com/futurealiti) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## Car model

The car uses “Ferrari-Testarossa-84-low-poly” by XGLOL235. The sandbox scales the model to 4.48 metres, bakes its materials, and animates the wheels while driving. The importer also tags the tail, brake, reversing, bumper and head lamps and the covers of the pop-up headlights, so the shaders can light the lamps and open the pods, and writes the pods' hinge, the 55-degree opening that the model's own animation gives them and the headlamp beams to `assets/testarossa/lamps.json`. Rebuild it with `python3 scripts/import_terrain_vehicle.py --vehicle testarossa /path/to/ferrari-testarossa-84-low-poly.zip` and gltfpack 0.18. [Original model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/ferrari-testarossa-84-low-poly-2eb128eab34a4c25bfcdb2d75152d31a) · [Author](https://sketchfab.com/XGLOL235) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## Aircraft model

The aircraft uses “F5” by manilov.ap. The sandbox uses a gear-up mesh scaled to 15.6 metres, centred on its centre of gravity, and packs the original paint textures into one atlas. The importer also tags the ailerons, stabilators and rudder and writes their hinges to `assets/f5/surfaces.json`, so the vertex shader can move them, and closes the holes in the source mesh that they would uncover. Rebuild it with `python3 scripts/import_terrain_vehicle.py --vehicle f5 /path/to/f5.zip`, gltfpack 0.18, and ImageMagick. [Original model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/f5-8089d66a8a8748b2a4b049b3c06f6a02) · [Author](https://sketchfab.com/manilov.ap) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## Race gate models

The road gate uses the board and metal tabs of “Finish Line” by Kemal Çolak. Solid posts on the 0.8-metre footprint of its lattice towers replace the towers and reach 8 metres below the base, so sloping ground never shows a gap under them. A flat label panel replaces the 3D lettering, and the board's back carries a copy of its printed face. The air gate is the same board and tabs at three times the size, without posts, so a jet flies through the space where the towers would be. The source has 29,658 triangles; the road gate has 1,976 and the air gate 1,960. [Original model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/finish-line-70aecb6bac234266bdebd22322396caa) · [Author](https://sketchfab.com/kemalcolak) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

The water gate uses the inflatable arch of “Davita Finish-Line 2020” by BrandXR, scaled to 12 metres wide with its opening 3.6 metres high. Its feet reach 2.7 metres below the water line: the gate rides at the wave height at its centre, and at the highest choppiness, 300%, the water at a foot can stand 2.5 metres lower. The source builds the legs and the crossbar as separate pieces, so the importer removes the 36 faces hidden inside the crossbar and closes the 3.5-centimetre grooves where the pieces meet, leaving 912 of the 940 triangles. Its prints are new: the importer never extracts the archive's texture. [Original model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/davita-finish-line-2020-003d816301084f719897503bdb4ffbc3) · [Author](https://sketchfab.com/brandxr) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

Rebuild the three meshes, `assets/gates/gates.json` and the license files with `python3 scripts/import_terrain_gates.py /path/to/finish_line.zip /path/to/davita_finish-line_2020.zip`. The importer reads only geometry from the archives, then runs `node scripts/generate-terrain-gates.mjs`, which paints the start, checkpoint, finish and start / finish prints into each mesh's atlas, one band per role. `npm run generate` also runs the generator, and `npm run check:generated` verifies the committed prints. `gates.json` records each gate's window, posts and extent in metres and the texture offset of each role's print. The lettering is Barlow Semi Condensed Bold from `games/php-billiards/art/typography`, under the SIL Open Font License 1.1.
