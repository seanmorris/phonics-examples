# Landscape Sandbox

An offline authoring and flight sandbox for the optional `terrain-phonics` package. Fly over procedural mountains, canyons, or coasts; shape the ground; grade roads; adjust the sun and atmosphere; drive a car, ride a buoyant watercraft, or fly an F5, and race each of them through gates you place.

See [the controls guide](docs/index.md) and [landscape authoring](docs/authoring.md).

The terrain, camera, editing, and simulation run in PHP. Browser code provides accessible authoring controls, file dialogs, input collection, and lifecycle integration. The example does not require Tiled or a network connection.

Run `node scripts/bench-terrain.mjs` from the examples checkout with the preview running to measure cold and warm road clicks and flight frame intervals. The report includes the GPU, drawing-buffer size, time to each confirmed marker, and time spent in the picker. Set `TERRAIN_URL` for another preview or `VIEW_WIDTH` and `VIEW_HEIGHT` for a different browser viewport.

While the view moves, the renderer selects terrain detail at most ten times a second. To compare frame rates without that limit, run `document.querySelector('canvas').terrainSandboxDebug.values({selectionInterval: 0})` in the browser console, or pass another spacing in seconds up to 1. The same object's `state.selectionInterval` reports the value in use and `state.renderer.selections` counts selections. The value has no control in the panel and is not saved with a landscape.

A selection that needs more than 3 milliseconds of work continues over the following frames and changes the drawn terrain in one of them. Set `selectionSliceMs` through the same `values()` call to give each frame more or less of that work, or to 0 to run every selection whole in one frame as before. `state.selectionSliceMs` reports the value in use, and `state.renderer` reports `selectionWorkMs` for the last frame, `selectionUpdates` for the frames the last selection took and `selectionInFlight` while one is under way. This value is not saved either.

The status object reports the race gates in `state.course`, each with its opening's `width` and `height` in metres, and the race in `state.race`. `state.race.reach` is null while the vehicle can pass the next gate's opening; otherwise its `message` says why not, and for the F5 its `needed` gives the opening height in metres the F5 needs. `state.gateSizes` gives each kind's own `width` and `height`, its `widths` and `heights` ranges, and the water arch's `stretch`. `state.renderer.gateDraws` counts the gates the last frame drew, after culling by distance and by the view, and `state.renderer.gateMs` is the time it spent on them in milliseconds. While you drive, ride or fly, `state.duck.offset` gives the metres [x, y, z] by which the drawn chase eye moved to clear a gate at the last step, and `state.duck.passages` the passage it holds past each nearby gate by id: `through`, `over` or `under`. `state.camera` stays the eye the vehicle's camera chose.

The Testarossa's brake and reversing lamps follow the pedals and the handbrake, and its tail lamps light with the headlights. **L** and a gamepad's **X** step the headlights through Auto, On and Off, and the **Car headlights** select sets them; the Drive on land section of [the controls guide](docs/index.md) gives the rules. While the car is driven with its headlights lit and the sun below the horizon, `VehicleModel::spotLights()` builds one light per headlamp from the pose each frame draws, and `application.php` passes them to the terrain package's `Scene::setSpotLights()` whenever they change. In every other case the application clears them; it reads the sun from the atmosphere's `nightVisibility`, which is zero while the sun is up and the beams would make no visible difference. A paused car takes no steps, so `Session::input()` snaps the headlamps and pods to a changed switch at once (`CarLamps::snap()`). `VehicleModel::BEAM_INTENSITY`, `BEAM_RANGE`, `BEAM_INNER` and `BEAM_OUTER` set each beam's intensity, range and cone; the intensity scales with how far the headlamps have lit, and stays at zero until the rising pods lift the headlamps clear of the nose (`BEAM_CLEAR`, which `tests/lamps.php` checks against the mesh). `state.headlights` reports the switch and `state.headlightsLit` whether it lights them now. While driving, `state.lamps` gives each lamp's level and the pods' opening. `state.renderer.spotLights` counts the lights set.

The panel's Surface tab sets the terrain package's `TerrainAppearance`: the ground's colours, banding and blending. PHP sends the settings' groups, ranges, defaults and `TerrainAppearance::USES` once through the `terrainSurface` bridge, and the page shows only the settings the current preset reads (`state.surfaceStyle`). The settings are ordinary saved settings; `Session::load()` builds the whole appearance from a file before it replaces anything.

## Watercraft model

The watercraft uses “Jetski” by Futurealiti. The sandbox uses a simplified mesh and a resized dashboard texture. To regenerate them, run `python3 scripts/import-terrain-jetski.py /path/to/jetski.zip` with gltfpack 0.18 and ImageMagick on the path. [Original model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/jetski-15e79c65f67a468592cdc2ac1f5b389b) · [Author](https://sketchfab.com/futurealiti) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## Car model

The car uses “Ferrari-Testarossa-84-low-poly” by XGLOL235. The sandbox scales the model to 4.48 metres, bakes its materials, and animates the wheels while driving. The importer also tags the tail, brake, reversing, bumper and head lamps and the covers of the pop-up headlights, so the shaders can light the lamps and open the pods, and writes the pods' hinge, the 55-degree opening that the model's own animation gives them and the headlamp beams to `assets/testarossa/lamps.json`. Rebuild it with `python3 scripts/import_terrain_vehicle.py --vehicle testarossa /path/to/ferrari-testarossa-84-low-poly.zip` and gltfpack 0.18. [Original model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/ferrari-testarossa-84-low-poly-2eb128eab34a4c25bfcdb2d75152d31a) · [Author](https://sketchfab.com/XGLOL235) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## Aircraft model

The aircraft uses “F5” by manilov.ap. The sandbox uses a gear-up mesh scaled to 15.6 metres, centred on its centre of gravity, and packs the original paint textures into one atlas. The importer also tags the ailerons, stabilators and rudder and writes their hinges to `assets/f5/surfaces.json`, so the vertex shader can move them, and closes the holes in the source mesh that they would uncover. Rebuild it with `python3 scripts/import_terrain_vehicle.py --vehicle f5 /path/to/f5.zip`, gltfpack 0.18, and ImageMagick. [Original model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/f5-8089d66a8a8748b2a4b049b3c06f6a02) · [Author](https://sketchfab.com/manilov.ap) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## Race gate models

The road gate uses the board and metal tabs of “Finish Line” by Kemal Çolak. Solid posts on the 0.8-metre footprint of its lattice towers replace the towers; each one's foot reaches a metre below the ground under it, so sloping ground never shows a gap. A flat label panel replaces the 3D lettering, and the board's back carries a copy of its printed face. The air gate is the same board and tabs at three times the size, without posts or base plates, so a jet flies through the space where the towers would be. The source has 29,658 triangles. At their own sizes the road gate draws 1,976 and the air gate 1,408; their files hold 4,344 and 5,760, with spare checker columns for wider openings that fold to nothing until a gate needs them. [Original model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/finish-line-70aecb6bac234266bdebd22322396caa) · [Author](https://sketchfab.com/kemalcolak) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

The water gate uses the inflatable arch of “Davita Finish-Line 2020” by BrandXR, scaled to 12 metres wide with its opening 3.6 metres high. Its feet reach 2.7 metres below the water line: the gate rides at the wave height at its centre, and at the highest choppiness, 300%, the water at a foot can stand 2.5 metres lower. The source builds the legs and the crossbar as separate pieces, so the importer removes the 36 faces hidden inside the crossbar and closes the 3.5-centimetre grooves where the pieces meet, leaving 912 of the 940 triangles. Its prints are new: the importer never extracts the archive's texture. [Original model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/davita-finish-line-2020-003d816301084f719897503bdb4ffbc3) · [Author](https://sketchfab.com/brandxr) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

Rebuild the three meshes, `assets/gates/gates.json` and the license files with `python3 scripts/import_terrain_gates.py /path/to/finish_line.zip /path/to/davita_finish-line_2020.zip`. The importer reads only geometry from the archives, then runs `node scripts/generate-terrain-gates.mjs`, which paints the start, checkpoint, finish and start / finish prints into each mesh's atlas, one band per role. `npm run generate` also runs the generator, and `npm run check:generated` verifies the committed prints. `gates.json` records each gate's window, posts and extent in metres at its own size, the range of opening sizes it takes, the layout of its board's checker bands and the texture offset of each role's print.

A placed gate's opening takes any width and height in its kind's range. The importer tags every vertex with the part it belongs to, and `assets/vehicles/model.vert` moves each part for the size: the posts move to the opening's sides and rise to its top, the board sits on top with its label at its own size and as many whole checker columns as fill each band, and the water arch scales, at most twice as much one way as the other. The road gate's posts and base plates also follow the ground under each post. The lettering is Barlow Semi Condensed Bold from `games/php-billiards/art/typography`, under the SIL Open Font License 1.1.
