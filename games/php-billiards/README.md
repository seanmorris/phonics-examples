# PHP Billiards

PHP Billiards is a local practice table and Matrix-backed two-player eight-ball game. PHP owns deterministic fixed-step ball physics, casual eight-ball rules, exact checkpoints, canonical table geometry, camera math, SDL input, the native control HUD, and the GLES 3 renderer. The browser bridge sizes the canvas, supplies fullscreen and sound, and forwards validated Matrix room events. The application runs directly from its PHAR and has no Tiled, world-composition, Doom, or WAD dependency. Its session and sixteen balls live in Phonics' ECS, demonstrating that the registry and scheduler work independently of those optional content systems; the ECS references the simulation's authoritative Ball objects without copying or reordering physics state.

The browser bridge claims the shared WebGL2 context with multisample antialiasing before SDL starts, so the PHP scene and native HUD render through the same antialiased framebuffer without supersampling the procedural materials.

Drag on the cloth to point the cue, or use the left stick or arrow/WASD keys; hold Shift or click the left stick for fine aim. Set power with up/down or the power meter, and draw down from the meter to pull the cue back and release to shoot. Drag the spin disc, or use Q/E or the triggers for top and draw and Z/C or the shoulders for side spin; X clears it. Space or A shoots, Backspace or B undoes the shot, and R or Y racks again. Right-drag or the right stick orbits the camera, the wheel zooms, and 0 or R3 puts the camera behind the cue. After a scratch, move the ghost cue ball with the pointer, keys, or stick; illegal spots explain themselves and are never sent online. Escape or Start opens the menu (sound, HUD, fullscreen, controls), H hides the HUD, and ? lists every control. The ? hint stays beside the menu button throughout play, even with the HUD hidden; on a controller it names the Start menu instead.

The HUD is drawn natively in PHP with a signed-distance atlas generated from Barlow Semi Condensed (SIL Open Font License), and the browser bridge plays recorded ball, cushion, and pocket samples. Inside a pocket, each strike against the walls plays one of three recorded knocks chosen by the strike's strength, so a ball rattling down the liner sounds different every time; reaching the bottom plays the sink recording.

The title menu offers **Free Play** and **Online Play**. Lobby chat and challenge links use standard Matrix text messages so other Matrix clients remain part of the conversation. Online tables use commit/reveal seeding and explicit sequenced Matrix relations; both players must acknowledge the same exact state hash before another action begins. The exact PHAR, runtime-manifest fingerprint, and rules version must match. See [the online protocol and determinism notes](docs/online.md).
